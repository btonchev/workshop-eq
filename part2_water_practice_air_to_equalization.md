# Water Practice --- Air → Equalization in the Water

**Duration:** ~50–60 min

**Purpose:** Applying equalization underwater from 0 to 10 meters.

---
## 1. WARM-UP — STATIC APNEA

**Duration:** 10 min

### Goal

Prepare for the in-water equalization session while maintaining relaxation, calmness and body awareness.

### Static Apnea — 2 Rounds

**Round 1 — Relaxed Static Apnea**
- Prepare calmly and breathe normally before the attempt.
- Perform a comfortable static breath-hold.
- End the attempt before discomfort becomes significant.
- Recover with normal, relaxed breathing.

**Round 2 — Relaxed Static Apnea**
- Allow full recovery before starting.
- Repeat a comfortable static breath-hold.
- Focus on relaxation and awareness rather than duration.
- Recover fully before beginning the equalization practice.

### Key Rules
- No hyperventilation.
- No competitive breath-holding or maximum-duration attempts.
- Both rounds must be supervised by a qualified instructor, with appropriate in-water safety.
- Do not begin the descent practice until fully recovered and comfortable.
---

## 2. IN-WATER EQ PRACTICE

**Duration:** 35 min

### Main Goal

Develop the participant's intuitive ability to restore the Working Volume after each equalization, allowing the next equalization to be performed smoothly and with minimal effort.

Throughout the descent from 0 to 10 metres, the participant explores how the larynx, glottis and tongue interact to restore the Working Volume and prepare for the next equalization.

The instructor provides feedback without prescribing every movement, allowing the participant to discover a repeatable and efficient coordination pattern.

### Key Principle

> Equalize → Restore Working Volume → Equalize Again.

The goal is not simply to equalize the ears, but to intuitively discover how to reset the system between consecutive equalizations.

Participants practise coordinating:

- **Glottis** — isolating the working air volume during compression.
- **Larynx** — compressing the working volume to generate pressure.
- **Tongue seal** — establishing and releasing the seal as required by the technique.

The objective is to repeat this cycle smoothly and efficiently throughout the descent, without forcing equalization.

How to restore working air volume?

### Round 1 — 3 Dives with the Instructor

**Focus:** Technique awareness and feedback.

Perform three controlled dives within the 0–10 m training range, according to the participant's ability.

During each dive, focus on:

1. **Glottis** — maintain isolation of the working air volume.
2. **Larynx** — practise controlled movement without pushing from the chest or abdomen.
3. **Tongue seal** — establish and maintain a consistent seal.

After each dive, receive instructor feedback and identify one point to improve.

### Round 2 — 3 Dives without Step-by-Step Instructor Prompting

**Focus:** Reproducing the technique independently.

Perform three controlled dives, applying the feedback from Round 1.

During each dive, focus on:

1. **Glottis** — maintain control of the airway.
2. **Larynx** — reproduce the controlled movement.
3. **Tongue seal** — maintain a reliable seal throughout the attempt.

After each dive, assess:

- Was the technique controlled?
- Was the seal maintained?
- Was equalization gentle and comfortable?
- Was there any need to force the movement?

### Key Takeaway

> The purpose of all six dives is to practise glottis control, laryngeal movement and tongue seal — not to reach a particular depth or produce maximum pressure.

### Safety Rules

- No hyperventilation.
- Never dive alone; maintain appropriate buddy coverage and qualified supervision during both rounds.
- Equalize early and gently.
- Never force equalization through pain or blockage.
- Stop the descent if equalization fails or discomfort occurs.
- Progress toward 10 m only when equalization remains comfortable and controlled.
