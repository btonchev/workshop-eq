# Part 2 — Water Practice

**Duration:** ~60–75 min

## Advanced Frenzel, Sequential Frenzel & Mouthfill

> **Purpose:** Transfer the advanced equalization mechanisms developed in Part 2 Dry into real water conditions.

The objective is not to increase depth.

The objective is to reproduce the same air-management mechanisms under increasing pressure while maintaining a relaxed descent.

> **Dry control → shallow water → repeatability → greater pressure → depth**

---

## 1. Practice Principles

- **No depth goal.** Quality comes before depth.
- **Equalize early.** Do not wait for discomfort.
- **Small and frequent.** Use the smallest movement that works.
- **Relaxation first.** Do not compensate for poor control with force.
- **One technique at a time.** Do not change equalization techniques during the same dive.
- **Progress gradually.** Increase pressure only when the mechanism is comfortable and repeatable.
- **Stop before pain or significant discomfort.**

> **Depth is the consequence of control, not the objective of the exercise.**

---

## 2. Surface Water Familiarisation

Before adding depth, reproduce each mechanism at the surface.

### 2.1 Advanced Frenzel — Reverse Pack

**Total breath-hold: 40–60 seconds.**

1. Start face down at the surface with a snorkel.
2. Take a comfortable breath.
3. Equalize the mask.
4. Remove the snorkel.
5. Keep the glottis closed.
6. Establish the Frenzel tongue position.
7. Practise a very small reverse pack.
8. Return to the normal working position.
9. Repeat without increasing effort.

### Focus

- Can you feel the larynx movement clearly?
- Can you keep the reverse pack small?
- Can you return to a stable working volume?
- Does the movement remain relaxed?

> **The objective is control of the refill, not maximum air volume.**

### 2.2 Sequential Frenzel — Reservoir to Working Chamber

**Total breath-hold: 40–60 seconds.**

1. Start face down at the surface with a snorkel.
2. Take a comfortable breath.
3. Equalize the mask.
4. Remove the snorkel.
5. Establish a small oral reservoir.
6. Keep the glottis closed.
7. Create the tongue seal.
8. Isolate the working chamber.
9. Transfer a small amount of air from the oral reservoir into the working chamber.
10. Compress the working chamber.
11. Perform a gentle equalization.
12. Reset the working chamber.
13. Repeat the transfer.

**ORAL RESERVOIR → WORKING CHAMBER → COMPRESS → EQ → RESET → REPEAT**

### 2.3 Mouthfill — Establish and Maintain

**Total breath-hold: 40–60 seconds.**

1. Start face down at the surface with a snorkel.
2. Take a comfortable breath.
3. Equalize the mask.
4. Remove the snorkel.
5. Establish the mouthfill.
6. Maintain the oral reservoir without excessive pressure.
7. Move a small amount of air into the working position.
8. Perform a gentle equalization.
9. Return the air to the reservoir.
10. Repeat while remaining relaxed.

**CHARGE → MAINTAIN → MOVE AIR → FRENZEL → RETURN → REPEAT**

---

## 3. Shallow Water Practice

Choose a shallow depth where the pressure change is noticeable but the technique remains comfortable.

### 3.1 Relaxed Descent Position

**Total breath-hold: 24–30 seconds.**

1. Start face down at the surface with a snorkel.
2. Take a comfortable breath.
3. Equalize the mask.
4. Remove the snorkel.
5. Descend slowly.
6. Let the body become vertical and relaxed.
7. Keep the head in a natural position.
8. Stay streamlined and allow yourself to hang in the water.
9. Return to the surface calmly.

> **The goal is to make the relaxed diving position feel natural — not to practise advanced equalization yet.**

### 3.2 Advanced Frenzel

**REVERSE PACK → WORKING VOLUME → FRENZEL → REPEAT**

Focus on small reverse packs, controlled larynx movement, early equalization, and relaxed jaw and neck.

### 3.3 Sequential Frenzel

**ORAL RESERVOIR → WORKING CHAMBER → COMPRESS → EQ → RESET → REPEAT**

Focus on maintaining the reservoir, small air transfers, clear separation between reservoir and working chamber, and repeatability.

### 3.4 Mouthfill

**CHARGE → MAINTAIN → MOVE AIR → FRENZEL → REPEAT**

Focus on establishing the mouthfill before pressure becomes demanding, maintaining the reservoir, and moving only the required amount of air.

---

## 4. Deeper Water Practice

Only progress when the shallow-water version of the technique is comfortable and repeatable.

Choose a depth that increases the pressure challenge without requiring maximal effort or forcing the technique.

> **We are increasing pressure to test air-management control — not to train maximum depth.**

### 4.1 Advanced Frenzel

As the available working volume decreases:

**REVERSE PACK → WORKING VOLUME → FRENZEL → REPEAT**

The reverse pack should remain small and controlled.

### 4.2 Sequential Frenzel

Maintain the oral reservoir and repeatedly transfer small amounts into the working chamber:

**RESERVOIR → TRANSFER → WORKING CHAMBER → COMPRESS → EQ → RESET**

The transfer may need to become more frequent as pressure increases. The mechanism does not change.

### 4.3 Mouthfill

Establish the mouthfill before the pressure becomes demanding.

**MAINTAIN RESERVOIR → MOVE AIR → FRENZEL → REPEAT**

The objective is to maintain enough usable air without continuously pressurising the entire mouthfill unnecessarily.

---

## 5. Equalization Timing

Once the mechanics are stable, practise the timing of equalization.

On separate short dives:

1. Equalize early.
2. Repeat at a comfortable interval.
3. Notice the pressure change between equalizations.
4. Keep the movement small.
5. Avoid waiting until discomfort appears.

**EARLY → SMALL → EASY → REPEATABLE**

> **Do not use increasing effort to compensate for late equalization.**

---

## 6. Position and Head Control

On separate dives, compare:

- neutral head position;
- slightly extended head position;
- slightly flexed head position.

Do not force any position.

### Buddy Observation

The buddy watches for:

- head movement;
- neck tension;
- jaw tension;
- shoulder tension;
- loss of streamlining;
- visible effort before equalization.

> **The diver should learn which position allows the easiest and most repeatable equalization.**

---

## 7. Short Repeated Dives

| Dive | Objective |
|---|---|
| **1** | Establish relaxed descent |
| **2** | Equalize earlier |
| **3** | Reduce force |
| **4** | Maintain stable head position |
| **5** | Repeat the easiest version |

Do not increase depth simply because the previous dive was successful.

---

## 8. No-Force Drill

If equalization does not work easily:

**STOP DESCENT → RELAX → RETURN SHALLOWER → RESET**

If it still does not work comfortably, terminate the dive.

> **A failed equalization is a signal to stop and reset — not a signal to push harder.**

---

## 9. Technique Comparison

Perform the three techniques on separate short dives.

| Observation | Advanced Frenzel | Sequential Frenzel | Mouthfill |
|---|---|---|---|
| Where did the working air come from? | | | |
| Was a separate reservoir maintained? | | | |
| How was the working volume replenished? | | | |
| How much air was transferred? | | | |
| How easy was the equalization? | | | |
| Where did unnecessary tension appear? | | | |

### The Three Strategies

**Advanced Frenzel**

> I replenish the working volume from the lungs using reverse packing.

**Sequential Frenzel**

> I transfer air from the oral reservoir into the working chamber.

**Mouthfill**

> I maintain a larger oral reservoir and use it progressively for equalization.

## 11. Common Problems

### Equalization requires more force

Possible causes:

- equalizing too late;
- too much pressure in the working chamber;
- excessive jaw or neck tension;
- loss of the tongue seal;
- loss of the working chamber;
- poor reservoir management.

Response:

**STOP → RELAX → RETURN SHALLOWER → RESET**

### Sequential Frenzel feels like Advanced Frenzel

Check the source of the air.

Sequential Frenzel should involve:

**ORAL RESERVOIR → WORKING CHAMBER**

It should not rely on a new reverse pack from the lungs for each equalization.

### Mouthfill is constantly pressurised

Reduce unnecessary pressure.

The goal is to maintain a usable reservoir, not to keep the entire mouth under maximum pressure.

### Technique breaks down with depth

Do not immediately add force.

Return to a shallower depth and reproduce the mechanism with smaller, easier movements.

---

## 12. Progression Criteria

Progress only when the current level is:

- comfortable;
- repeatable;
- relaxed;
- technically consistent;
- free of pain or significant discomfort.

**DRY CONTROL → SHALLOW WATER → REPEATABILITY → GREATER PRESSURE → DEPTH**

> **Do not progress because a depth was reached once. Progress because the mechanism remains controlled and repeatable.**

---

## 13. What This Practice Should Achieve

By the end of this practice, the diver should be able to:

- reproduce Advanced Frenzel in water;
- use small reverse packs without excessive effort;
- distinguish Sequential Frenzel from Advanced Frenzel;
- transfer air from an oral reservoir into the working chamber;
- establish and maintain a Mouthfill;
- equalize early and frequently;
- maintain a relaxed head, jaw and neck;
- recognise when technique is deteriorating;
- stop rather than force an equalization.

The final progression is:

**UNDERSTAND THE MECHANISM → CONTROL IT ON LAND → FEEL IT IN SHALLOW WATER → MAKE IT REPEATABLE → ADD PRESSURE → ONLY THEN ADD DEPTH**

> **Depth is not the exercise. Air management and control are the exercise.**
